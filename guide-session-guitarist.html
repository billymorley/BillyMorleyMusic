How to brief a session guitarist.

The information that helps a professional guitarist prepare the right parts, equipment and schedule for your project.

By Billy Morley · Practical guide

Start with the purpose of the job

Explain whether you need a live performance, recording session, dep, rehearsal or touring guitarist. Say whether the existing parts should be reproduced closely or interpreted.

Send dates and schedules first

Include the main date plus rehearsals, travel, load-in, soundcheck and expected finish time where known. Mark anything that is still provisional.

Share the music clearly

Send current tracks, a numbered set list, charts and notes about key changes, live arrangements, endings, tunings and important guitar parts.

  • Current audio or video references
  • Set list in running order
  • Charts or chord sheets
  • Rehearsal and performance dates
  • Equipment and backline details

Explain rehearsals and preparation

State how many rehearsals are planned, where they are and what should be ready before the first one. For dep work, explain whether there will be a full rehearsal, a short run-through or none.

Include equipment information

Mention amplifiers, pedals, in-ear monitoring, supplied backline, silent-stage requirements and any specific guitar or tuning needs.

Be clear about the commercial details

Preparation, rehearsals, performance or recording time, travel, accommodation and usage can affect the fee. Clear information prevents changes later.

Simple briefing checklist

Service: live, studio, dep, rehearsal or tour
Dates: performance, rehearsal and travel
Location: venue, studio or routing
Music: links, set list, charts and notes
Equipment: supplied backline and requirements
Contact: one person confirming details

Good information leads to better preparation

A useful brief does not need to be long. It needs to be current, organised and honest about what is still undecided.
