What does a tour manager do?

A straightforward look at the planning and on-the-road work that keeps an artist’s tour moving.

By Billy Morley · Practical guide

The tour manager turns plans into a workable day

A tour manager helps the artist and crew move through each show day with clear information and practical support. The exact role changes with the size of the tour.

Advancing the shows

Advancing means confirming access, parking, load-in, timings, technical contacts, hospitality, merchandise terms and settlement arrangements before arrival.

Building itineraries and schedules

The touring party needs one current source of information covering addresses, contacts, travel, hotels, soundcheck, doors, stage time and curfew.

Travel and accommodation

Tour management may include routes, transport, rooming lists, hotel access, parking and realistic departure times.

Venue and promoter liaison

The tour manager is often the central contact between the touring party and local teams, directing questions to the right person and keeping the artist focused on the show.

Settlements, expenses and merchandise

Depending on the agreement, the role can include show settlements, receipts, expenses, merchandise figures and end-of-night reconciliation.

Problem solving on the road

Traffic, delayed travel, technical issues and last-minute changes happen. The job is to assess priorities, communicate clearly and protect the next commitment.

Information to send a tour manager

Dates, routing, venues, touring party size, transport, accommodation, production contacts, existing crew roles and the responsibilities you need covered.

The goal is a calmer, more consistent tour

Good tour management does not remove every problem. It provides a reliable system for identifying, communicating and resolving them.
