Tour management

Clear plans, clear communication and fewer surprises.

Touring involves a large number of moving parts, and small problems become expensive when information is unclear or decisions are delayed. My role is to help keep the artist, crew, venues and suppliers working from the same plan.

Support can begin before the first show with advancing and preparation, then continue on the road with schedules, logistics, settlements, merchandise and day-to-day problem solving.

Tour support

Practical help across the full run.

Show advancing

Confirming venue contacts, timings, access, technical details, hospitality and local arrangements.

Itineraries and schedules

Creating clear daily information for travel, hotels, load-in, soundcheck, doors, show and curfew.

Travel and accommodation

Helping coordinate routes, transport, parking, hotels and practical requirements for the touring party.

Venue liaison

Acting as a central point of contact between the artist, crew, promoter, venue and local staff.

Settlements and paperwork

Supporting show settlements, documentation, receipts, expenses and agreed reporting.

Merchandise

Helping organise stock, venue arrangements, sales information and end-of-night reconciliation where required.

On the road

Calm decisions when plans change.

Even a well-advanced tour will change. Traffic, venue access, technical issues, delayed travel and last-minute requests all need to be handled without creating unnecessary stress for the artist or crew.

A good tour manager keeps track of the wider picture, communicates what matters and deals with problems in the right order.

The exact responsibilities can be agreed around the size of the tour, touring party, transport setup and existing production team.

Tour management FAQs

Planning the right level of support.

Do you work on short tours?

Yes. Enquiries are welcome for one-off shows, short runs and longer projects, subject to availability.

Can you advance the shows?

Advancing can be included where venue, promoter and production contacts are supplied in good time.

Do you drive?

Transport and driving requirements should be included in the initial enquiry so the role and schedule can be assessed properly.

What should I send first?

Send the dates, routing, number of people, transport plan, venues, artist requirements and the responsibilities you need covered.

Tour enquiries

Send the routing and responsibilities.

The clearer the initial brief, the easier it is to assess availability and the support required.
