How to choose your first guitar.

What beginners and parents should check before buying an electric or acoustic guitar.

By Billy Morley · Practical guide

Choose around the music you want to play

You do not have to begin on acoustic guitar before moving to electric. If electric-guitar music is what motivates you, an electric instrument may be the better start.

Comfort matters more than the logo

Check that the body, neck and overall size feel manageable. You should be able to reach the first fret without straining your shoulder or wrist.

Check the action

The action is the distance between the strings and frets. Very high action makes a guitar unnecessarily difficult; very low action can cause buzzing. A basic setup can transform an affordable instrument.

Make sure it stays in tune

Tune the guitar, play for a few minutes and check it again. Turn the machine heads and make sure they move smoothly.

Inspect used guitars carefully

  • Look for cracks, neck damage or a lifting bridge.
  • Check every fret for dead notes and excessive buzzing.
  • Plug in an electric guitar and test every pickup and control.
  • Leave room in the budget for strings and a setup.

Budget for the complete setup

An electric player may need an amplifier, cable, strap and headphones. An acoustic player still needs a tuner, case, picks and spare strings.

First-guitar checklist

Choose electric or acoustic around the music you enjoy. Check size, comfort, string height, tuning stability and condition. Most importantly, choose an instrument that makes you want to practise.

A modest guitar can be an excellent first instrument

You need a playable, comfortable guitar that stays in tune. Consistent practice and good guidance matter more than buying the most expensive instrument available.
